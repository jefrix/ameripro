ENERGY ARCHITECTURE / INITIAL BASELINE

One load bus. Two sources.

The baseline uses an interlocked source selector and dedicated generator-fed battery charger. Generator and inverter outputs are not assumed to operate in parallel.

BATTERY MODE

Engine-off operation

The inverter carries eligible loads while battery energy remains above the selected reserve. Fans and thermal-management loads still consume energy.

GENERATOR MODE

Supply and recharge

The generator carries site load and a controlled charger. Charging backs off as load rises, subject to selected equipment limits.

FUTURE OPTION

Parallel microgrid

Peak shaving, grid interaction and seamless transfers require a different validated controls / protection design. No summed output rating is claimed here.

DryForce fleet integration

The current DryForce study allocates 19.7 kW of connected real power. Its voltage, phase, starting demand and protective interfaces are unresolved. The preset below is an energy-planning case, not a compatibility approval.

An external-power DryForce variant would retain its equipment protections and replace or omit its onboard generation only after coordinated design. Do not subtract its generator weight or cost from the existing design until that variant is defined. The diesel-driven MFP-500 does not become an electrically driven pump by connecting this trailer.

Equations and limits

DC energy window = nominal kWh × (upper SOC − reserve SOC) / 100. Usable AC energy = DC window × discharge efficiency. Average demand = site average + auxiliaries. Engine-off hours = usable AC energy / average demand.

Charger AC input = minimum(charger input limit, maximum(0, generator kW × dispatch fraction − average demand)). Recharge hours = DC window / (charger AC input × charge efficiency). Long-run generator-on equivalent = 24 × recharge hours / (recharge hours + engine-off hours). This is a repeated-cycle equivalent, not the actual first calendar day's engine runtime.

The model checks peak running real power plus auxiliaries against both sources independently. It does not model peak duration, charger transients, kVA, voltage dip, power factor, phase imbalance, motor starting or battery discharge C-rate. No fuel savings, fuel runtime or uninterrupted transfer capability is predicted.
