1. Site arrangement basis

Treat the two trailers as independently deployable systems with separate water and air boundaries. Positioning, clearances and routes must be established from a site-specific assessment. The site diagram is not to scale.

BoundaryProposed arrangementEvidence required before a site plan is approved
Water sourceScreened intake connected to MFP-500 suctionSource classification, intake access, elevations, debris and suction analysis
Water destinationPump discharge connected to authorized receiverReceiver acceptance / capacity, route, disposal requirements and hose restraints
Drying zoneDryForce supply and return connected to an assessed building zoneContamination assessment, isolation, moisture survey and airflow plan
Regeneration airSeparate outdoor inlet and wet-air outletSupplier airflow / pressure limits and termination assessment
Engine / powerDedicated service access and exhaust / cooling arrangementSelected equipment installation requirements and electrical design
Site accessReserved access route between working areasActual clearances, pedestrian / vehicle routes and service / emergency access

Drying does not automatically start when pumping starts or ends. Establish the permitted sequence, water control, isolation and contamination handling before air circulation. Completion depends on the restoration plan and recorded material conditions.

Where the fleet illustration differs, the Revision B 20 ft / 24 ft bodies and mass ceilings govern.

3. Candidate cause-and-effect matrix

This matrix defines review questions and intended functional responses. Setpoints, time delays, signal integrity, independent protections, fault priorities and reset rules remain unresolved. Protective functions are local; telemetry does not replace them.

CauseCandidate effectRecovery questionVerification evidence
Pump source below allowed levelInhibit pumping outside the supplier-approved envelope; local alarm / recordWhat level recovery, hysteresis, off-time and restart permission are required?Pump limits, level-sensor failure analysis and approved restart test
Engine protective faultRetain OEM shutdown / derating behavior; present local faultWhich faults require inspection and local reset?OEM engine interface, alarm map and supplier-approved test
Regeneration airflow absent or lostPrevent heater energization or disable heat; alarmWhat fan run-on / purge and restart checks are required?Airflow-proof design, heater protective architecture and failure test
Drying package overtemperatureDisable affected heat through supplier-specified protection; alarmWhich cooling / fan functions must remain available, and how is reset authorized?Limit-device design, priority logic and supplier-approved trip test
Invalid / stale humidity signalFlag invalid demand; no assumed continuation on last valueIs a bounded fallback permitted, or must the affected process stop?Range / plausibility logic, timeout and signal-failure test
Remote communications lostLocal protective functions remain effective; log / indicate lossDoes the selected site require continued local operation or a stop? How are stale commands rejected?Network-loss and recovery test without unintended restart

Normal stop, emergency stop, protective trip and complete loss of power must each receive a defined sequence. For a heated drying package, do not assume that cutting all power is interchangeable with disabling heat and providing supplier-required cooldown. The final behavior belongs in a coordinated supplier / controls design.
